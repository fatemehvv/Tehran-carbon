Dear Editor,

We submit the research article "Reach, more than the graph, explains the skill of distance-based graph neural networks in
mapping Tehran's NO₂ hotspots from TROPOMI" for consideration in *Earth Science Informatics*.

Graph neural networks are increasingly used to map urban emissions and air pollution from satellite and urban data,
and their gains are usually credited to the graph. A graph network, however, also draws on a larger neighbourhood
than the baselines it is compared with. We separate the two for monthly TROPOMI NO₂ hotspots in Tehran: spatial-lag
models and the same network without a graph are given the GNN's reach, and reach is varied for all of them under
spatial block cross-validation and on a held-out year. Skill levels off near the correlation length of the target; at
equal reach the graph adds little, and the network fed neighbourhood means is statistically equivalent to it in
Spearman correlation. We also show how seed averaging and the choice of inference change the verdicts for a target
with about five independent cells, and recommend reach-matched baselines for graph models in spatial environmental
mapping.

We believe the article fits the journal's focus on informatics methods for the Earth sciences: it concerns how
data-driven spatial models built on satellite observations should be evaluated.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and both authors have approved its submission. The authors
declare no competing interests. Supplementary material is provided as Online Resource 1.
