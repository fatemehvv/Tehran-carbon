# Review 2 (ChatGPT)

## A. Summary

The paper asks whether adding an urban data source improves a model of Tehran’s TROPOMI NO₂ pattern because of the source’s spatial information or because the model receives extra inputs and, in gated fusion, another encoder. It compares real energy, traffic and monitoring-station inputs with spatially rearranged versions under the same architecture, using 12 spatial folds and a held-out year. All six energy Spearman point estimates favor the real inputs, while all six estimates for the joint traffic-plus-stations comparison favor the surrogates. Neither surrogate construction passed the registered texture check. The paper also tests target support using wind-selected seasonal composites and Gaussian smoothing, but the wind contrast failed its manipulation check and the registered smoothing contrast was inconclusive.

**Review scope.** I received the Markdown manuscript only. It contains the three figure captions and image paths and a summary of the registration in §3.6. The figure files, original registration and companion manuscript were unavailable, so I could audit the text, tables and captions, but could not verify plotted values, registration timing or the full extent of companion-paper overlap.

## B. Recommendation

**Reject in its present form.** The title promises separation of architecture from information, but both controls failed the criterion that makes that interpretation credible, and their agreement does little to rule out shared bias. The target-support analysis cannot independently carry the paper because the intended sharpening failed and the smoothing result is inconclusive under a design that fixes predictor reach. A substantially narrower paper on the failure of spatial placebo controls could be publishable with calibration and a complete reproducibility package.

## C. Major comments

1. **Both placebos failed the check that supports the paper’s central interpretation.** The title, Highlights 2–3, §3.2 opening, §5.1 and §6 claim that the control separates architecture from information. In §4.4, **zero of five** realizations passed for each construction. The reported median maximum correlogram discrepancies are **0.21–0.36** for A and **0.10–0.20** for B against a tolerance of **0.05**. Both constructions can disrupt the urban core, break the relationship with the retained context and provide a stable spatial pattern that the model can exploit. Agreement across A and B shows that the observed signs recur under two rearrangements of the same data. It does little to rule out shared bias or establish its direction. Put M4 before the model results, flag every affected contrast, and report them as sensitivity analyses. To retain the separation claim, require a surrogate that passes prespecified, target-blind checks on the **actual model inputs** within the 2,436-cell label mask and across folds. Include feature distributions, directional spatial dependence, within-source covariance and relationships with retained predictors. Calibrate the full procedure on a known-null simulation with a single urban core and on a positive control with a known added signal. A donor city is a candidate for testing. Its core, mask, covariate relationships and sampling still need validation.

2. **The contrasts answer narrower questions than the paper assigns to them.** In §4.2, Table 2’s placebo-minus-context comparison changes the inputs and the model together. It estimates the effect of adding **that particular surrogate input** with the associated model change. It does not identify a generic architecture effect. In early fusion there is one encoder (§3.1), so the comparison changes the inputs and input-layer parameter count without adding a branch. The energy placebo also retains real heating degree-days (§3.2). The fixed-architecture real-minus-placebo comparison is potentially useful, but it also changes how energy covaries with context, especially given the shared inventory proxies in §2.2 and the high lineage \(R^2\) values in Table 4. Report parameter counts and absolute model scores. Add a parameter-matched context-only baseline, a gated branch with constant inputs and the same degree-day handling, and a simple spatial baseline such as coordinates or a smooth spatial trend. For incremental information beyond context, test a fold-fitted, context-conditioned surrogate or a residual-based control and check what it actually preserves. Finally, F2/C2 replace **traffic and stations jointly, given energy and context**. Run separate contrasts to support source-specific claims, or remove those claims from Highlight 4, the Abstract, §4.4 and §6. Table 4 provides no power-plant-only placebo comparison either.

3. **Narrow the novelty claim and document the companion-paper overlap.** The strongest candidate for novelty in §1 is retraining an otherwise identical multimodal network with a spatial surrogate in one encoder. The broader idea has a close precedent. Ridder, Hardy and Ovaskainen, *Generating spatially realistic environmental null models with the shift-&-rotate approach helps evaluate false positives in species distribution modelling* (DOI `10.1111/2041-210X.14443`), refit models with shifted and rotated environmental predictors and assess false positives against known simulated truth. Mrkvička et al., *Revisiting the random shift approach for testing in spatial statistics* (DOI `10.1016/j.spasta.2020.100430`), document the spatial-correlation problem caused by toroidal wrapping. Hessel and Lee, *Does my multimodal model learn cross-modal interactions?* (DOI `10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-main.62`), address a related attribution problem through a prediction diagnostic. Whether the exact matched-encoder application has appeared before is **unsure**. Cite the closest precedents and explain what retraining a matched branch adds to them. [besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.14443?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

   The target-support experiment is a specific application, with limited methodological novelty. Friedrichs-Manthey et al., *From topography to hydrology* (DOI `10.1002/ece3.6110`), study the impact of spatial support on model results. Goldberg et al., *Evaluating the spatial patterns of U.S. urban NOₓ emissions using TROPOMI NO₂* (DOI `10.1016/j.rse.2023.113917`), already use low-wind selection and oversampling. Here the potentially distinct question is whether the **incremental gain from fusion** varies with target support. Sections 1, 2.1, 3.1 and 5.5 already attribute the prior fusion result, target dependence, network and null calibration to the companion paper. Supply that manuscript confidentially to the editor and provide a crosswalk of shared data, folds, models, predictions and conclusions. The visible overlap is substantial enough to require disclosure. Its extent is **unsure** without the companion. [onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ece3.6110?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

4. **Calibrate inference for these folds and define the verdicts.** Sections 2.1 and 3.4–3.5 give a target correlogram zero crossing of about 24 km, 22-km blocks, a 2-km buffer and \(2^{12}\) sign assignments. Exhaustive enumeration gives the exact distribution **under the assumed sign-flip scheme**. Its validity requires the joint null distribution to be invariant to the allowed flips. Adjacent test blocks, shared training observations and repeated monthly maps make that an empirical issue. The target’s effective sample size of about five per month is also a different quantity from the effective sample size of the **paired score contrast**. Most importantly, §5.5 reports that the companion’s null calibration found the Spearman test liberal. Bring that result into the inference section and calibrate the actual pipeline used here. Publish fold sizes and maps, the precise rank statistic, what is flipped, how dates are grouped, whether ranks are recomputed on deletion, whether the jackknife refits models, and how unequal blocks are handled. Show sensitivity to block origin, buffer and influential folds. If calibration remains poor, report the intervals and tests as exploratory. Holm adjustment cannot repair miscalibrated individual \(p\)-values. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24530839/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

   In Table 1, the two B rows with Holm \(p<0.05\) have jackknife intervals crossing zero. The A/1403 interval excludes zero, while Holm \(p=0.056\). This can happen with different statistics or decision rules. The manuscript currently presents them as one verdict without specifying how they fit together. State the null, tail, threshold, interval rule and precedence for each label, and publish every raw and adjusted \(p\)-value with the exact family membership by year and construction, including F4. Justify why 0.02 matters in this application. No reported Table 1 interval fits within ±0.02, and no energy interval has a lower bound above +0.02. The wide F2/C2 intervals allow both useful gains and losses. Report **26 of 30 matched seed comparisons** as an optimization diagnostic. They reuse data and pair seed with placebo realization, so they are neither 30 independent confirmations nor a clean measure of seed variability. Show the seed-by-realization results and specify how predictions and scores were averaged. Keeping PR-AUC descriptive is appropriate. Apply that choice in the Highlights, Abstract and Discussion as well. [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

5. **The smoothing experiment confounds target support with predictor reach and changes how training and test labels are constructed.** Sections 2.2, 3.3 and 4.3 hold the input windows at roughly 7.6 km while target smoothing reaches Gaussian \(\sigma=12\) km. A Gaussian standard deviation and a square-window half-width are different measures of support. Training labels are smoothed around fold-specific holes, while test labels are smoothed over the complete domain, so their effective kernels differ near boundaries. The test truth can also incorporate nearby training observations. Define the intended prediction task and quantify the discrepancy between these two label constructions near fold edges. State whether the initial monthly standardization, as well as the final one, uses training cells only during fitting. Cross target smoothing with predictor reach, include the simple spatial baseline under the same folds, and report absolute scores alongside gains. For F4, report the raw gain contrast as a sensitivity analysis. Dividing by \(1-\rho_{\text{energy}}\) makes the relative contrast unstable near one. Section 4.3 confirms that several edge blocks were dropped from the sign-flip test for this reason. Identify those blocks, give the actual number of assignments and explain why the jackknife and test target the same quantity, if they do. The registered estimate of −0.180 with a 95% interval of −0.549 to +0.189 is inconclusive.

6. **M2 failed to establish a sharper target. Section 5.3 overinterprets why.** In §4.3, the calm composites have shorter zero crossings by **1.3 and 0.8 km**, and lower correlations at 8–10 km by **0.003 and 0.022**. Neither year meets either registered threshold. The supported result is that these composites did not achieve the prespecified sharpening. The claims in §5.3 that daily transport does not set the pattern at this scale and that a sharper target requires another instrument or oversampling are unsupported by this design. Section 3.3 selects relative wind thirds with 15–29 days per group, then represents each season by its middle month. Show actual wind speeds and directions, coverage on a common mask, uncertainty in the spatial diagnostics, and how weather, station CO and heating degree-days were aligned with each set of contributing days. Define the monthly reference used for M1 and the splitting unit for M3. Keep F3 uninformative under the reported rule. Also correct the attribution in §§1, 3.3 and 5.3. Liu et al., DOI `10.5194/acp-24-3717-2024`, use overpass-time winds averaged vertically in a flux-divergence analysis. The manuscript should identify the passage supporting its calm-day compositing and seasonal coverage claims or revise those citations. Goldberg et al., DOI `10.1016/j.rse.2023.113917`, provide the direct precedent for stagnant-day selection. [acp.copernicus.org](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/3717/2024/acp-24-3717-2024.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

7. **Define the spatial and temporal validation tasks.** Section 3.4 calls 1404 “new places in a new year.” These are withheld blocks of the same city, with 1403 inventory and traffic layers reused in 1404 (§2.2). That tests transfer to a new year with stale inputs. It cannot establish performance with a current inventory or in a new city. Show a like-for-like one-year-lag sensitivity in the development years if earlier layers exist. For station CO, §2.2 says dropping stations near the test block “avoids pre-processing leakage.” Document the station-to-grid procedure in each fold and when station data would be available in deployment. Explain whether the real and surrogate station surfaces are built from the same allowed stations **before** relocation, and whether neighborhood features may cross the buffer. Overlapping windows of externally available covariates are not inherently leakage, but the folds must reflect what is known at prediction time. Section 2.1 also defines the label mask by positive inventory CO₂. State which inventory vintage sets that mask and whether the result is sensitive to an inventory-independent geographic mask.

8. **Make the registration auditable and distinguish contingencies from amendments.** Section 3.6 reports M4 failure, and §4.3 reports the M2-triggered exclusion of F3. Those are candid disclosures. By the manuscript’s own account, running B after A failed and removing F3 after M2 failed were planned contingencies. Calling the B run a “Deviation” blurs that distinction. Conversely, the F4 edge-block exclusions (§4.3), any separate Holm families for A and B, year-specific F1/F2 tests, early-fusion comparisons, secondary architecture estimates and the 1404 smoothing run need explicit provenance. Supply the immutable registration, a dated record of when results and the held-out year were first examined, and a planned-versus-executed table with the reason and timing for each change. Label new analyses exploratory. The supplied Markdown does not establish that any specific post-hoc choice was presented as registered, and it does not establish that the reported analysis followed the original protocol.

9. **The paper is not reproducible as submitted.** “Data and code availability” gives local paths, promises an archive on acceptance and refers to the companion study for the data. Supply a review-accessible repository or archive pinned to the analysis commit, an environment specification and commands that regenerate the tables and figures from fold-level predictions. Provide the block masks, seed and surrogate identifiers, M1–M4 diagnostics and disclosure of data licenses or restrictions. For restricted inputs, provide a concrete access route and shareable derived results or fixtures where permitted. Sections 2.1 and 3.3 need the exact satellite product and band, QA rules, regridding, observation weights, coverage and missing-data handling, and the rule for taking logs of nonpositive values. If the source is Earth Engine’s OFFL L3 NO₂ collection, its tropospheric band can contain negative values. Explain the relationship between the analysis grid, retrieval footprint and effective spatial support. The target throughout is a relative **tropospheric column pattern**. Sections 1 and 6 should keep interpretations within that scope, especially when discussing emissions or hotspots. [developers.google.com](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_S5P_OFFL_L3_NO2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## F. Questions to the authors

2. **§§3.2, 3.5 and Table 1:** Were all five seeds run for every placebo realization? Do the reported intervals condition on those five surrogate draws, and what happens when each realization is omitted in turn?
3. **§§3.5–3.6 and Table 1:** What null produces the listed one-sided \(p\)-value, what rule produces the verdict, and which comparisons were in each actual Holm family?
4. **§§2.1, 3.3 and 3.4:** Which preprocessing steps could see held-out cells or months, including target scaling, station interpolation, the CO₂ mask and surrogate construction?
5. **§1, §3.1 and Data and code availability:** Which predictions and results were reused from the companion study, and which can an independent reviewer regenerate from the materials available at submission?

## G. What would change the recommendation

I would reconsider a paper that demonstrates a prespecified, calibrated matched-branch control and shows that its result survives reasonable spatial validation choices. That revision would need to establish what the surrogate preserves on the model’s actual inputs, support any source-specific claim with a source-specific contrast, and make the registration and analysis reproducible during review.

A credible alternative is a paper centered on **why spatial placebo branches fail in a bounded city**. It would need to show, using known-null and known-signal experiments, when those failures produce misleading model comparisons and whether a practical diagnostic can detect them. Either version should treat the existing wind result as a failed sharpening experiment and keep the smoothing conclusion conditional on the tested predictor reach and model.
