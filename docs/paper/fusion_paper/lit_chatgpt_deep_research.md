## Target spatial support and the marginal value of predictors or fusion

**Zhalehdoost, A., & Taleai, M. (2025). “Unravelling the importance of spatial and temporal resolutions in modeling urban air pollution using a machine learning approach.” *Scientific Reports*, 15, 27708. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-13639-3. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-13639-3).** They deliberately aggregated Tehran emission-inventory fields to 500, 750, and 1000 m and retrained RF/MLP models, finding pollutant-dependent resolution effects; this is geographically and conceptually very close, but it varies the modeled spatial grid rather than asking whether **the incremental gain from adding/fusing distinct data domains** changes as the *response support* is coarsened. citeturn233866search2turn827370view0

**Tian, T., Helbich, M., Yuan, Z., Vermeulen, R., Hoek, G., & Kerckhoffs, J. (2025). “Assessing the role of spatial aggregation schemes with varying campaign durations of mobile measurements on land use regression models for estimating nitrogen dioxide.” *Environmental Pollution*, 368, 125689. DOI: 10.1016/j.envpol.2025.125689. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envpol.2025.125689).** They aggregated 5.7 million mobile NO₂ observations onto road segments and grid cells from 25–200 m and retrained linear and random-forest LUR models, directly demonstrating that the spatial support of the *response observations* changes apparent model performance, but they did not test whether a particular predictor domain or multi-source fusion becomes more valuable as support changes. citeturn195616search3turn195616search4

**Friedrichs-Manthey, M., Langhans, S. D., Hein, T., Borgwardt, F., Kling, H., Jähnig, S. C., & Domisch, S. (2020). “From topography to hydrology—The modifiable area unit problem impacts freshwater species distribution models.” *Ecology and Evolution*, 10(6), 2956–2968. DOI: 10.1002/ece3.6110. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.6110).** This is the clearest cross-domain precedent for the underlying experiment: the authors changed response/predictor support across ten spatial resolutions and explicitly showed that **predictor importance changes with resolution**, but it is ecological species-distribution modeling rather than multisource air-pollution fusion. citeturn233866search0turn233866search5

**Goldberg, D. L., Tao, M., Kerr, G. H., Ma, S., Tong, D. Q., Fiore, A. M., Dickens, A. F., Adelman, Z. E., & Anenberg, S. C. (2024). “Evaluating the spatial patterns of U.S. urban NOx emissions using TROPOMI NO₂.” *Remote Sensing of Environment*, 300, 113917. DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2023.113917. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2023.113917).** They explicitly contrasted the spatial structure of TROPOMI composites under low-wind versus higher-wind conditions and used stagnant-day composites to compare ~1-km urban NOx inventory patterns with satellite NO₂; this is an excellent precedent for using wind selection to alter effective spatial smearing/support, but they did **not** retrain a fusion model and test whether predictor-domain gains change between calm and windy targets. citeturn195616search6turn184952search5

## Pre-training redundancy measures that predict fusion gain

**Liang, P. P., Cheng, Y., Fan, X., Ling, C. K., Nie, S., Chen, R. J., Deng, Z., Allen, N., Auerbach, R., Mahmood, F., Salakhutdinov, R., & Morency, L.-P. (2023). “Quantifying & Modeling Multimodal Interactions: An Information Decomposition Framework.” *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems*, 36. arXiv:2302.12247; conference DOI: 10.52202/075280-1192. [arXiv URL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12247).** This is the closest conceptual precedent: it computes partial-information-decomposition quantities—redundancy, modality-unique information and synergy—between input modalities and the target **before training candidate models**, then uses those quantities to recommend unimodal, early-fusion or late-fusion approaches; however, the quantities are not defined at a geospatial target support such as a 1-km grid. citeturn852880search0turn852880academia7turn852880search8

**Zhang, T., Ding, R., Luong, K.-D., & Hsu, W. (2025). “Evaluating an information theoretic approach for selecting multimodal data fusion methods.” *Journal of Biomedical Informatics*, 167, 104833. DOI: 10.1016/j.jbi.2025.104833. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbi.2025.104833).** They directly tested whether pre-model PID redundancy/uniqueness/synergy statistics predict downstream fusion-model behavior over seven biomedical modality pairs, finding only partial consistency, so this is very close to your “can redundancy forecast fusion gain?” question but not spatial, environmental, or scale-aware. citeturn940248search0turn940248search1

The important novelty boundary is therefore quite narrow: **pre-training, target-conditioned redundancy as a predictor of fusion usefulness is already a published idea**, whereas computing that redundancy after aggregating each source to the *same physical support as the satellite response* and testing whether it forecasts fusion gain in spatial environmental ML appears not to have been established in the papers I found. citeturn852880search8turn940248search0

**Verdict: partly done.** The general method is done before; the target-support-aware geospatial version appears novel in this search. citeturn852880search0turn940248search0

## Spatial placebo or knockoff modalities as a fusion control

**Wagner, H. H., & Dray, S. (2015). “Generating spatially constrained null models for irregularly spaced data using Moran spectral randomization methods.” *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*, 6(10), 1169–1178. DOI: 10.1111/2041-210X.12407. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12407).** They developed Moran spectral randomization to create surrogate variables that preserve multiscale spatial autocorrelation, including multivariate randomization that can preserve within-group correlation structure; this supplies almost exactly the **null-data generator** your placebo branch would need, but it was designed for spatial hypothesis testing rather than insertion as a bogus modality into a trained fusion architecture. citeturn903831search0turn903831search1

**Burt, J. B., Helmer, M., Shinn, M., Anticevic, A., & Murray, J. D. (2020). “Generative modeling of brain maps with spatial autocorrelation.” *NeuroImage*, 220, 117038. DOI: 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117038. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117038).** BrainSMASH generates surrogate spatial maps with matched variograms/spatial autocorrelation while breaking their original topographic correspondence, which is extremely close to your proposed “same distribution and spatial smoothness, wrong location” placebo construction, but the surrogates are used to form null distributions for spatial associations rather than as an extra branch in multimodal prediction. citeturn934209search0turn903831search3

I found no verified 2015–2026 paper in which an autocorrelation-preserving but deliberately mislocated spatial source is passed through an otherwise identical multimodal branch so that **real source + extra branch** can be contrasted against **placebo source + extra branch** to identify an architectural branch benefit separately from informational benefit. The spatial-null methodology needed to implement such a control is mature, but the fusion-control experiment itself appears missing from the searched literature. citeturn903831search0turn934209search0

**Verdict: not found.** The closest precedents are Wagner & Dray (2015) and Burt et al. (2020), which provide the surrogate-map machinery but not the placebo-modality fusion experiment. citeturn903831search0turn934209search0

## Stability and validity of gate or attention weights

**Vulli, S., Liu, M., & Huang, Z. (2026). “Reliability-Aware Cross-Modal Fusion of Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 Optical Imagery for Robust Patch-Level Land Cover Scene Classification.” *Remote Sensing*, 18(15), 2484. DOI: 10.3390/rs18152484. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18152484).** This is an unusually close geospatial precedent: the model learns SAR/optical modality gates, reports five-random-seed experiments, explicitly states that average gate weights vary across seeds, and tests whether weights respond appropriately when one modality is degraded or when a branch is correct, while also documenting a heavy-noise regime in which gate behavior becomes unstable. citeturn827370view4turn657796view0

**Madhyastha, P., & Jain, R. (2019). “On Model Stability as a Function of Random Seed.” In *Proceedings of the 23rd Conference on Computational Natural Language Learning (CoNLL)*, 929–939. DOI: 10.18653/v1/K19-1087. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/K19-1087).** They systematically quantified how random initialization changes attention-, gradient-, and LIME-based interpretations, showing that attention explanations can be inconsistent across seeds; this is not modality gating or geospatial modeling, but it directly establishes seed stability as a necessary interpretability diagnostic. citeturn841617search2

**Wiegreffe, S., & Pinter, Y. (2019). “Attention is not not Explanation.” In *Proceedings of EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019*, 11–20. DOI: 10.18653/v1/D19-1002. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D19-1002).** They proposed explicit tests of whether attention is explanatory, including a variance calibration based on repeated random-seed runs and comparisons against uniform/adversarial attention, so the methodological principle of validating attention weights rather than reading them literally is firmly established. citeturn841617search3

The 2026 Sentinel-1/Sentinel-2 paper means this is no longer merely an adjacent NLP idea: a remote-sensing fusion study has now performed both multi-seed evaluation and empirical checks on whether learned modality weights track modality reliability, although it does not use air-quality sources such as traffic, emissions and stations. citeturn657796view0

**Verdict: done before.** Vulli, Liu & Huang (2026) is the closest direct precedent; Madhyastha & Jain (2019) and Wiegreffe & Pinter (2019) establish the broader attention-stability methodology. citeturn657796view0turn841617search2turn841617search3

## Shared spatial proxies in emission inventories and air-quality predictors

**Geng, G., Zhang, Q., Martin, R. V., Lin, J., Huo, H., Zheng, B., Wang, S., & He, K. (2017). “Impact of spatial proxies on the representation of bottom-up emission inventories: A satellite-based analysis.” *Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics*, 17, 4131–4145. DOI: 10.5194/acp-17-4131-2017. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-17-4131-2017).** They created six NOx inventories with the same total emissions but different allocation proxies—including population, nighttime lights, road/vehicle information and industrial GDP—and quantified how those proxy choices altered agreement with satellite NO₂, directly demonstrating that a gridded “emission” field can inherit much of its spatial structure from socioeconomic surrogate variables. citeturn749590search0

**Gately, C. K., & Hutyra, L. R. (2017). “Large Uncertainties in Urban-Scale Carbon Emissions.” *Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres*, 122(20), 11,242–11,260. DOI: 10.1002/2017JD027359. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017JD027359).** They explicitly quantified spatial correlations between emission inventories and population at multiple resolutions, finding that inventory–population correlation depends strongly on sector and scale; this is the strongest paper I found for **quantified redundancy caused by proxy allocation**, although it concerns fossil-fuel CO₂ rather than NOx and does not then feed both emission and population fields into the same ML model. citeturn827370view5turn848364view3

**Mu, Q., Denby, B. R., Wærsted, E. G., & Fagerli, H. (2022). “Downscaling of air pollutants in Europe using uEMEP_v6.” *Geoscientific Model Development*, 15, 449–465. DOI: 10.5194/gmd-15-449-2022. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-15-449-2022).** Their high-resolution European downscaling explicitly tests alternative residential-combustion allocation proxies based on population density, building density, and combinations/masks of the two, showing that population and built form are themselves ingredients used to construct spatial emission fields rather than necessarily independent information sources. citeturn749590search2turn338165search5

**Goldberg, D. L., Tao, M., Kerr, G. H., Ma, S., Tong, D. Q., Fiore, A. M., Dickens, A. F., Adelman, Z. E., & Anenberg, S. C. (2024). “Evaluating the spatial patterns of U.S. urban NOx emissions using TROPOMI NO₂.” *Remote Sensing of Environment*, 300, 113917. DOI: 10.1016/j.rse.2023.113917. [Working URL](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2023.113917).** Their ~1-km NEMO inventory is produced from 108 spatial surrogates and is evaluated directly against low-wind TROPOMI NO₂, providing a modern urban-NOx example in which gridded emissions are explicitly understood as spatially disaggregated proxy products rather than purely independent measurements. citeturn195616search2turn158774search2

These papers establish both pieces of your concern—emission inventories inherit population/building/road patterns, and that inherited similarity can be quantified—but I did not find a study that explicitly asks, within an urban NO₂ ML predictor set, “how much apparent information in the emission-inventory domain is duplicated because the same population/building proxy also appears as a separate predictor?” and then quantifies that redundancy at the model target scale. citeturn749590search0turn848364view3turn749590search2

**Verdict: partly done.** Proxy-induced dependence and inventory–population correlations have been quantified, but the specific “inventory plus its own allocation proxy as separate ML domains” redundancy diagnosis appears not to have been carried through in the closest air-quality studies I found. citeturn749590search0turn827370view5

## Overall novelty verdict

For **target support versus fusion gain**, the verdict is **partly done**: response/grid-scale sensitivity and even Tehran-specific multi-resolution ML exist, and low-wind TROPOMI compositing exists, but I found no paper that tests how the *incremental benefit of adding or fusing domains* varies with target support. citeturn233866search3turn195616search3turn195616search6

For **pre-training target-scale redundancy as a predictor of fusion gain**, the verdict is **partly done**: Liang et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2025) already use pre-training target-conditioned redundancy/synergy to anticipate suitable multimodal modeling, but I found no geospatial implementation tied explicitly to the physical support of the response raster. citeturn852880search0turn940248search0

For **an autocorrelation-preserving misplaced placebo source inside a fusion branch**, the verdict is **not found**: spatially constrained null generators are well established, but I found no verified paper using such a surrogate as an otherwise identical extra modality branch to distinguish architectural benefit from genuine information. citeturn903831search0turn934209search0

For **seed stability and validity of fusion gates/attention weights**, the verdict is **done before**: Vulli, Liu & Huang (2026) explicitly examine remote-sensing modality-gate behavior across five seeds and test whether gate weights track modality reliability, with the broader methodological rationale established by 2019 attention-interpretability studies. citeturn657796view0turn841617search2turn841617search3

For **shared allocation proxies and quantified redundancy in urban emissions/air-quality inputs**, the verdict is **partly done**: the emissions literature explicitly shows that population, roads, nighttime lights and building density can generate inventory spatial patterns and has quantified inventory–population correlations across scales, but I found no close NO₂ ML paper that converts this into a formal target-scale redundancy analysis between the inventory domain and the same proxy supplied independently as a predictor. citeturn749590search0turn848364view3turn749590search2
